A Precision-Oriented Retrieval-Augmented Legal Compliance Assistant

An evidence-grounded baseline for legal question answering and a local company-policy review demonstration

Global Compliance Intelligence projectVersion 0.3November 2026Manuscript draft · Not peer-reviewed

Abstract

Organizations need to find applicable legal requirements, inspect their own policies, and preserve enough provenance for reviewers to check the analysis. A fluent language-model answer alone does not establish that a provision is authoritative, current, or supportive of the claim. This paper presents Global Compliance Intelligence, a research prototype for evidence-grounded legal question answering and a local, single-company policy-review demonstration. The legal pipeline accepts PDF and DOCX files, extracts selectable text, preserves recognized legal headings as section metadata, stores document records in SQLite, and indexes dense vectors in embedded Qdrant. A separate collection and registry store fictional G Inc Markdown policy drafts. The review workflow retrieves legal and company evidence independently, optionally asks an OpenAI model for structured findings, and checks that returned evidence identifiers belong to the retrieved set. This check verifies identifier provenance, not semantic entailment. The current system is a dense-retrieval baseline with one Indian DPDP-focused demo scope; it does not implement hybrid search, reranking, multi-agent orchestration, cross-jurisdiction comparison, production tenant security, or regulatory monitoring. We define a reproducible evaluation protocol using retrieval, citation, faithfulness, abstention, latency, and cost measures. No quantitative results are reported because the legal corpus and adjudicated question set are not yet complete.

Keywords: legal information retrieval; retrieval-augmented generation; compliance; evidence provenance; citation verification; evaluation
Research status. This is a prototype report and proposed empirical study. It does not claim measured legal accuracy, a completed multi-agent system, or a validated compliance product.

Highlights

  • Legal and fictional company documents use separate registries and vector collections.
  • The local G Inc comparison checks citation IDs but does not prove semantic support.
  • The paper defines a controlled protocol for testing dense, hybrid, and future multi-agent retrieval.

1. Introduction

Legal and compliance work involves more than finding text that resembles a question. A useful research assistant must preserve the source, provision, version, and temporal context of its evidence. It must also be able to say when the indexed corpus does not answer the question. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) conditions a language model on retrieved documents [1], but retrieval alone does not guarantee completeness or prove that a cited passage supports a claim.

Global Compliance Intelligence is intended to grow into a platform for multi-jurisdiction legal research and company-policy review. The current codebase implements a narrower, inspectable slice: dense legal retrieval and a local G Inc comparison demonstration. This paper documents that scope, describes the present design, and proposes an evaluation that can test later improvements without assuming that additional model calls improve accuracy.

Research questions
  1. How well does dense retrieval return human-labeled relevant legal sections?
  2. Does hybrid retrieval with rank fusion and reranking improve retrieval and citation measures?
  3. Does bounded multi-agent orchestration improve faithfulness enough to justify its latency and cost?
  4. How reliably does the system abstain when evidence is weak, missing, contradictory, or temporally inapplicable?

2. Related work

Lewis et al. describe RAG as a combination of parametric generation and non-parametric retrieved knowledge [1]. Sentence-BERT uses siamese and triplet network structures to generate sentence embeddings that can be compared using cosine similarity [2]. Reciprocal Rank Fusion combines ranked results without requiring raw retrieval scores to share a common scale [3]. These works motivate the dense baseline and planned hybrid comparator; they do not establish performance on this project's legal corpus.

LegalBench is a collaboratively built benchmark that treats legal reasoning as a collection of distinct tasks rather than a single capability [4]. The proposed evaluation focuses specifically on legal-section retrieval, citation support, evidence-grounded answers, and abstention. It is not a replacement for a broader legal reasoning benchmark.

3. System design

3.1 Local architecture

The documented local setup uses FastAPI, SQLite, embedded Qdrant, and a React/Vite frontend. Redis is optional and is not used for background jobs. FastAPI exposes separate versioned routes for legal documents, questions, and the G Inc demo workspace.

3.2 Legal document ingestion and metadata representation

The legal endpoint accepts PDF and DOCX files. It applies a size limit, hashes content with SHA-256 for duplicate detection, extracts selectable text (including paragraph and table structures), and splits it using recognized legal headings and paragraph boundaries.

To ensure citation reliability and avoid ungrounded vector search, every indexed chunk is attached to a comprehensive metadata payload:

  • section: The exact statutory heading (e.g., Section 8(1), Chapter II - Obligations of Data Fiduciary, Rule 5) preserved during structural parsing.
  • document_title: The verified title of the act, regulation, or rule.
  • document_id: Foreign key linked to the persistent SQLite registry.
  • jurisdiction: ISO/statutory code (e.g., IN for India).
  • country & domain: Topical classification tags (e.g., India, data_privacy, cyber_law).
  • source_url: Direct hyperlink to the authoritative official gazette or government publication.

3.3 Metadata-anchored retrieval and anti-hallucination safeguards

Retrieval operates through a two-stage filter-and-rank pipeline rather than unconstrained global cosine similarity:

  1. Hard Metadata Pre-Filtering: Before computing vector distance, Qdrant executes payload filters. The search space is strictly partitioned by collection (legal_knowledge vs. company_knowledge) and pre-filtered by jurisdiction (e.g., jurisdiction == "IN"). Irrelevant or out-of-jurisdiction provisions are excluded before scoring.
  2. Dense Cosine Ranking: The query is embedded via sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 with normalize_embeddings=True, and candidate chunks are ranked by cosine similarity against the question vector.
  3. Similarity Threshold Abstention: If the top-scoring excerpt falls below the calibrated similarity threshold (0.25), the retrieval engine triggers explicit abstention (evidence_sufficient = False). The system informs the user that indexed evidence is insufficient rather than generating a speculative answer.
  4. Deterministic Citation Verification: In the compliance review workflow, the language model outputs structured findings referencing assigned evidence identifiers (L1, C1). The backend deterministically validates that all returned IDs exist in the retrieved candidate set; unrecognized IDs are discarded, and unsupported findings are downgraded to an insufficiency status.

3.4 Separate G Inc evidence and local LLM reasoning

Five synthetic Markdown drafts are provided for fictional G Inc: privacy, retention, incident response, vendor management, and access/security. Their example retention periods and response targets are invented company choices, not legal deadlines. They are indexed only through the company-demo endpoint into company_documents, company_sections, and the company_knowledge vector collection.

The local review endpoint retrieves legal and company evidence independently. When an LLM is enabled—either locally through Ollama running quantized open models (such as qwen2.5:3b or qwen2.5:7b with 4-bit quantization on a consumer GPU like an RTX 5050 8GB VRAM) or remotely via OpenAI—it executes a structured gap analysis. Findings are categorized into addressed_in_part, potential_gap, missing_company_evidence, or insufficient_legal_evidence. If the LLM is offline, the service gracefully returns the separated evidence panels without failing.

3.5 Security and legal-use boundary

The G Inc workflow is a local demonstration, not production tenant isolation. It has no login, organization membership, role-based authorization, or audit logging. Its endpoints are disabled when APP_ENV=production, but this is not production tenant security. Do not expose the demo to a network or use confidential documents.

4. Proposed evaluation

4.1 Systems to compare

  1. Dense baseline (implemented): query embedding → dense retrieval → top-*k* excerpts → optional generation.
  2. Advanced retrieval (planned): metadata filters → dense and sparse search → Reciprocal Rank Fusion → cross-encoder reranking → sufficiency check.
  3. Multi-agent RAG (planned): structured query understanding → validated retrieval plan → parallel jurisdiction retrieval → evidence aggregation → reasoning → bounded critic loop → citation checks.

Systems 2 and 3 are proposed comparators, not implemented features. Agents must have narrow responsibilities, typed outputs, bounded retries, and no authority to construct arbitrary database filters or bypass authorization.

4.2 Corpus and annotation

The initial corpus should contain a small, versioned set of official Indian DPDP materials. A downloader is provided for the Act, 2025 Rules, commencement notification, and corrigendum, but the PDFs are not currently present in this workspace. The Rules must be read with the corrigendum and relevant commencement instrument. This is a focused privacy demo, not a complete collection of Indian law.

Build questions for provision lookup, definitions, scope, exceptions, temporal applicability, policy comparison, and insufficient-evidence cases. At least two annotators with legal research experience should label relevant provisions, acceptable citations, expected answer points, and appropriate abstention. Adjudicate disagreements, report inter-annotator agreement, and reserve a held-out test set.

5. Implementation and verification status

The prototype implements metadata-anchored dense retrieval for legal Q&A, isolated G Inc company policy ingestion and retrieval, and an automated compliance review engine supporting both local Ollama models (such as Qwen 2.5) and OpenAI endpoints.

The indexed legal corpus currently contains 195 statutory sections across three official Indian instruments:

  1. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 22 of 2023, 55 sections).
  2. Information Technology (Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures and Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules, 2011 (G.S.R. 313(E), 45 sections).
  3. Information Technology Act, 2000 (Act No. 21 of 2000, 95 sections).

Verification of the codebase in the current environment confirmed:

  • Structural chunking and heading preservation unit tests pass (3/3).
  • React/TypeScript frontend builds cleanly (tsc -b && vite build).
  • Single-command setup and deployment scripts (setup.sh, start.sh) utilize uv and npm for fast, reproducible dependency management on Linux environments.
  • Live retrieval verified successful section-level grounding with cosine similarity scoring, threshold-based abstention, and citation generation.

No human-annotated legal evaluation dataset or formal quantitative precision/recall benchmark has been executed yet; those measurements are defined in the Section 4 protocol.

6. Limitations and conclusion

The parser's heading patterns are heuristic. The embedding model and threshold have not been fine-tuned on specialized legal vocabularies. Identifier checks confirm citation provenance rather than semantic entailment. The company workflow is a single local demonstration tenant without production authorization or encryption at rest.

This work provides a source-grounded legal retrieval baseline and a dual-corpus company policy review demonstration, together with an empirical evaluation protocol. Its contribution is an inspectable prototype running on local consumer-grade GPU hardware via Ollama and a testable research plan.
