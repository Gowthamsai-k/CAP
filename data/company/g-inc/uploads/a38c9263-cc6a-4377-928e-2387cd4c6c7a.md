# G Inc Data Retention and Deletion Standard

**Document class:** Synthetic company standard draft — not law  
**Company:** G Inc (fictional)  
**Version:** 0.1 (demo draft)  
**Owner:** Fictional Privacy Lead and Records Owner  
**Review date:** 2026-11-01  
**Approval status:** Unapproved; created only for software demonstration

> The durations below are invented G Inc demo settings. They are not statutory retention periods, legal advice, or evidence that a real organization meets its obligations.

## 1. Rule

Every system holding personal data must have a named owner, a documented purpose, a retention trigger, and a deletion or review action. Keep data only while the documented business purpose, contract, or approved recordkeeping basis requires it. A legal hold or other binding preservation requirement suspends routine deletion for the affected records; counsel must document the scope and release of the hold.

## 2. Illustrative schedule for the demo

| Record group | Demo retention choice | Trigger and action |
|---|---:|---|
| Unsuccessful job applications | 12 months | After the recruitment process closes, then delete or irreversibly de-identify |
| Employee personnel records | 7 years after employment ends | Review record category and any applicable hold before deletion |
| Customer support tickets | 24 months after resolution | Delete or remove personal identifiers unless the record is on hold |
| Routine authentication and access logs | 12 months | Rotate or delete after the period; retain incident evidence separately when authorized |
| Vendor due-diligence records | 5 years after relationship ends | Review contract and hold status, then delete or archive with restricted access |

These example durations are deliberately explicit to make demo gap analysis possible; G Inc’s owners and counsel would have to replace or approve each one for a real business.

## 3. Deletion workflow

System owners must maintain an inventory of scheduled records and deletion mechanisms. At least quarterly, the Records Owner should review overdue records, exceptions, failed deletions, and legal holds. Deletion should cover active systems and establish a documented expiry process for backups where immediate physical erasure is not technically available. Deletion logs must record the system, data category, date, action, and any approved exception without recreating the deleted personal data.

## 4. Exceptions and approval

Exceptions require a written reason, data scope, approving owner, review date, and compensating access controls. A team must not extend retention merely because storage is inexpensive or the data might be useful later. The fictional Privacy Lead and counsel review legal holds and disputed retention needs.
